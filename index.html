Less is more

A research instrument measured 192 surface proteins on every immune cell of COVID-19 patients. A hospital flow cytometer can read only a handful. How much of the information about disease severity survives when we shrink the panel?

Scroll to begin

01

It starts on a hospital ward

Spring 2020. A patient arrives with a positive SARS-CoV-2 test. Some patients recover at home; others end up in intensive care. At admission, it is hard to tell which way it will go.

Clinicians grade COVID-19 on a severity spectrum: from asymptomatic, through mild and moderate, to severe and critical.

We split symptomatic patients into two groups: non-severe (mild + moderate) and severe (severe + critical). Asymptomatic patients are left out of this binary task.

We keep one initial sample per patient to avoid counting the same person twice. That leaves 76 patients: 53 non-severe and 23 severe.

From each of them, the study took something small but extremely informative: a tube of blood.

02

Zooming into a drop of blood

The immune cells floating in the blood (PBMCs, peripheral blood mononuclear cells) are separated from red cells and plasma.

Under the microscope, it is a crowd: T cells, B cells, NK cells, monocytes, dendritic cells… They all look roughly the same: small, round, unremarkable.

Yet each cell type, and each state a cell can be in (resting, activated, exhausted), shows a different set of proteins on its surface.

So let's pick one cell and look at its surface.

03

Reading the surface with CITE-seq

The cell membrane is covered with proteins: CD markers. CD4 and CD8 mark helper and killer T cells, CD14 marks monocytes, CD19 marks B cells…

CITE-seq mixes the cells with a cocktail of antibodies. Each antibody grabs one specific protein.

The trick: every antibody carries a short DNA barcode. Sequencing the barcodes tells us how much of each protein sat on that cell. These are the Antibody-Derived Tags (ADTs).

The study panel had 192 antibodies, measured on every single cell, together with the cell's full RNA profile. Hover a marker to inspect it.

04

From cells to patients

Repeat this for every cell from every donor in the atlas of Stephenson et al. (Nature Medicine, 2021) and you get a matrix of 647,366 cells.

Each row also holds the RNA profile: 24,737 genes. A great deal of information, collected by an instrument no hospital routinely uses.

Four of the 192 antibodies are isotype controls: they bind nothing human and only measure background noise. We drop them, leaving 188 candidate proteins.

Finally, we summarise all the cells of each patient into one profile. That gives 76 rows, each labelled non-severe or severe, ready for a classifier.

    The questions we are answering

    RQ1

    Panel compression

    What is the smallest set of ADT markers that separates severe from non-severe COVID-19 nearly as well as the full panel, and which markers are they?

    In progress
    RQ2

    Specificity

    Is the signal specific to symptomatic COVID-19, or does it also show up in asymptomatic, LPS-challenged and non-COVID respiratory patients?

    In progress
    RQ3

    Modality

    Does RNA add predictive information beyond surface proteins?

    In progress
    RQ4

    Clinical translation

    Are the retained markers available in, or commonly used for, clinical flow cytometry?

    In progress
